# Bloc District

## A climbing gym that earned revenue before it opened

**Role:** UX/UI Designer & Developer (freelance) · **Duration:** 112 h · **Collaboration:** Estudio Bengala (branding) · **Platform:** WordPress + Elementor responsive

---

### The challenge

Bloc District needed a functional digital presence before the physical opening of their new climbing gym in central Barcelona. The goal was to turn the website into the business's first acquisition tool: generate visibility, position the brand against established competitors and — ambitiously — bring in revenue before the gym opened its doors. Digital development ran in parallel with the physical construction of the centre, which demanded extreme agility and constant reprioritisation.

---

### UX Research: knowing the user before the product exists

#### Personas built from qualitative data

Since the physical product didn't yet exist, we couldn't draw on real usage data. I designed a qualitative research approach centred on **segmentation forms by climbing level** (beginner, intermediate, advanced) distributed to potential users during the pre-sales phase. This strategic resource provided direct data on motivations, expectations and barriers before the opening.

From this, I built **two detailed personas**: Luca (22, engineering student, intermediate climber looking for community and technical progression) and Aida (30, social worker, beginner climber looking to expand her social circle and improve her physical and mental wellbeing). Each persona included goals, frustrations, personality traits and favourite apps, giving the team a concrete framework for design and communication decisions.

#### Prototype validation and qualitative testing

Prototypes were validated with representative users from each profile. Qualitative tests confirmed that the communication tone needed to balance sporting energy with accessibility for beginners — avoiding language that was too technical or intimidating, which would put off profiles like Aida.

#### Segmentation for future content

The data collected in the forms didn't just feed the initial design: it laid the foundations for the social media content strategy, where content was generated specifically for each of the three skill-level segments, with positive results in engagement.

---

### Design process

#### Two-week MVP sprint

The first phase was resolved in a two-week sprint: landing page with pre-sales enabled, registration form and payment gateway. The decision to include the payment system from the MVP — something the client hadn't originally asked for — was my own proposal, and it turned out to be key for validating real demand before the opening.

#### Iterative evolution in short sprints

In subsequent phases, the site grew in a modular way: versions in three languages (Spanish, Catalan, English), ERP integration, conversion statistics, database export and a booking system. Each feature was added in response to a detected need, not as a pre-conceived addition.

#### Coordination with branding and physical construction

I worked in collaboration with Estudio Bengala (responsible for branding) and in direct coordination with the gym's founders. Digital development ran in parallel with the construction of the centre, which required constant communication and the flexibility to adjust priorities week by week.

---

### Strategic approach: revenue before opening

- **Acquisition:** Social media previews drove traffic to the landing before the opening. The landing became the central hub for all pre-launch communication.
- **Activation:** Attractive visual design with a clear user experience. The MVP with payment gateway allowed early purchase of tickets and memberships at launch pricing.
- **Retention:** Customer list export features facilitated follow-up and direct communication with the first registered users.
- **Revenue:** Returns came sooner than expected, with subscriptions in the first two weeks — validating demand before the physical opening.

---

### Results

- Optimised landing with Elementor, ERP integration, working payment gateway and multilingual system.
- Positive revenue before the gym's physical opening.
- Early validation of interest in the service, reducing the financial risk of the launch.
- Segmented database by climbing level, usable for post-opening marketing and content.

---

### What I learned

This project showed that **a digital product can generate real value before the physical service exists**, if the prior research correctly identifies user motivations and the design aligns acquisition and monetisation from day one. Building personas from real qualitative data — not from assumptions — was what allowed fast, well-grounded decisions in a context of high uncertainty and tight deadlines. It also reinforced the importance of proposing features with a business perspective (such as the payment gateway in the MVP) beyond the original brief.
