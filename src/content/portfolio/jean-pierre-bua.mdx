# Jean Pierre Bua

## Luxury fashion e-commerce redesign in Barcelona

**Role:** UX/UI Designer · **Duration:** 310 h · **Team:** Marketing, styling, development · **Platform:** WooCommerce responsive

---

### The challenge

Jean Pierre Bua is a luxury fashion retailer based in Barcelona. Their e-commerce platform needed a full redesign to improve conversion in a highly competitive market. The audience was demanding — accustomed to premium digital experiences — and the catalogue ran to over 1,600 products. The main challenge was creating an aspirational experience that guided users intuitively towards purchase, without losing the sense of exclusivity the brand needed to convey.

---

### UX Research: what we learned from users

#### Audit and stakeholder interviews

The starting point was a **thorough audit of the existing platform** combined with **in-depth interviews with internal stakeholders** (management, marketing, styling, customer service). These sessions uncovered critical friction points in the user journeys and aligned the business's strategic expectations with the real needs we were observing.

#### Quantitative data analysis

I implemented **Google Analytics** to map drop-off points in the purchase funnel, identify pages with the highest bounce rates and understand real navigation patterns. The data revealed that users were abandoning at key moments in their journey: navigation through categories was confusing given the scale of the catalogue, and the product page failed to convey the urgency or exclusivity needed to close the sale.

#### Lean UX hypothesis validation

I adopted a **Lean UX methodology** to formulate hypotheses about user behaviour and validate them iteratively. Rather than designing from internal assumptions, each design decision was framed as a measurable hypothesis: "If we simplify brand navigation, we will reduce category abandonment by X%." These hypotheses were tested with prototypes before moving to development.

#### B2C buyer persona

The research defined a customer profile motivated by visual exclusivity, speed of navigation and an aspirational proposition consistent with a luxury standard. This user values apparent simplicity — they don't want to see technical information or stock levels — moves between mobile and desktop, and expects an experience as carefully crafted as the brands they buy.

---

### Design process

#### Information architecture

The complete restructuring of the navigation was one of the central pillars of the project. With over 1,600 products, it was essential to reorganise categories, filters and hierarchies so users could find what they were looking for in as few clicks as possible. The result was an architecture that prioritised the aspirational experience of the end consumer: clean, visual, without noise.

#### Modular design system

I developed a **design system** that ensured visual consistency across all screens and devices. The system included reusable components, a clear typographic hierarchy and a palette aligned with the brand identity. This allowed the development team to implement efficiently and maintain coherence as new sections were added.

#### High-fidelity prototypes

Iterative development of high-fidelity prototypes in Figma allowed design decisions to be validated before each development sprint. Each iteration was presented to the marketing and development team to gather feedback and adjust. Prioritising CTAs and strategically structuring interactions were key to directing user journeys towards conversion.

---

### Strategic approach: the conversion funnel

The intervention was structured around the funnel stages:

- **Acquisition:** Reorganisation of categories and optimised content architecture significantly improved organic SEO ranking.
- **Activation:** Clear navigation, CTA hierarchy and more direct product journeys reduced friction in the first interactions with the store.
- **Retention:** Work was done on a scalable foundation for catalogue management, making seasonal updates easier.
- **Revenue:** Improvements in acquisition and activation directly impacted interaction metrics and session quality.

---

### Results

- Increase in organic acquisition through SEO improvements.
- Higher quality interaction metrics (time on page, pages per session).
- New navigation architecture, consistent design system and iteratively validated UX/UI prototypes.
- A solid foundation for the future evolution of the platform.

---

### What I learned

This project reinforced a key conviction: in luxury fashion e-commerce, **hypotheses validated with real users carry more weight than internal assumptions**. Iterating from genuine need — rather than corporate intuition — was what made the difference in every design decision. It also confirmed that a flexible architecture needs to be accompanied by clear user segmentation, otherwise conversion becomes diluted.
